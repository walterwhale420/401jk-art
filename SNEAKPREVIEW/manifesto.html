02 // The Manifesto

Retirement is a joke. We refuse to be the punchline.

The deal

Work forty years. Put the life you wanted on hold. Make money for the 1% while you feed your pension account every month and trust that the math works out when you need it.

The fine print

While we were at work, someone changed the terms. Your employer used to promise a pension for life. Now you get an investment account and a prayer that the market is in a good mood the year you retire. The risk moved to you. The fees stayed with the bankers, who get paid whether you retire or not.

Progress was supposed to fix this. Machines, software and now AI squeeze more out of every hour, which should buy shorter weeks and earlier retirements. Instead the retirement age goes up again and again while pension payouts shrink. And not only in the US: more than half of OECD countries have a higher retirement age written into law. Tech got smarter. Somehow we work longer.

It isn't broken

Calling the system broken lets it off the hook. It does what it was built to do: keep most people working, paying and waiting while the returns flow to whoever already owns the assets. Wages crawl while rent and stock prices soar. The 1% wins no matter how the economy is doing.

Why we laugh

Nobody reads a white paper. Everybody reads a meme. A good one packs a whole argument into an image your friend gets in two seconds.

That's why the Jester leads. At court, the jester was the only one who could tell the king the truth and keep their head, as long as it came with a punchline. They made our retirement plan a joke. Now we're punching up and laughing back.

Why a meme coin

Crypto has always run on belief. A coin is worth what enough people decide it's worth. And for true meme coins the shared belief and the community are the utility. They're a place for people to belong, and their holders work for their bags because the meme's story resonates.

If you're thirty, renting, and your pension forecast says you "should be fine" at 74 as long as the market cooperates, you already know how this ends if nothing changes.

That's where 401jK comes in: a small amount you can afford to lose, on something that could go a long way. It's an asymmetric bet, and we take it with our eyes open.

What we believe

  • Freedom is claimed, not begged for at 74.
  • Community beats compliance.
  • We look up, not left or right. The ultra wealthy profit every time we fight each other.
  • 401jK is a plan B, not the whole plan. Don't YOLO your emergency fund.
  • Yes, there's a chart. But it's awareness first, hype second, then price.

What we refuse

The OG meme coins had communities that held through ugly years and carried a joke further than anyone expected. Then launchpads made it possible to mint a coin in thirty seconds, and the trenches filled with traps. By now over 99% of meme coins are pump-and-dumps or rug pulls. Deployed at breakfast, dead by lunch.

401jK isn't that. The dev walked away, the community took over, and the people holding it are the people running it. No cabal, no paid shills. We think a community that holds together can build something real, and we're here to prove it.

Retirement is a joke. We refuse to be the punchline.
